

# INTRODUCTION

Information used to prepare this publication was collected from Colorado's public school districts.

Many student membership tables contained in this publication are similar to those published in prior years. The dropout rate included in Table 2 is for grades 10-12 only. Additional information on dropout and graduation rates is available in a separate publication titled Colorado Graduation Rates for Class of 1995 and 1994-95 Annual Dropout Rates for Grades 7-12.

# HIGHLIGHTS:

- In fall 1995, there were 656,279 students in Colorado's public schools, an increase of 15,758 students (2.5%) over the fall 1994 membership of 640,521. Fall membership grew by 64,632 students (10.6%) from fall 1991 to fall 1995.
- Beginning with fall 1990, membership each year has surpassed the previous membership peak of 1972. Colorado's public school membership declined 5.6 percent between 1972 and 1983 before beginning an upward trend. From fall 1984 to fall 1995 student membership increased by 110,852 students (20.3%).
- Membership continues to rise in both elementary and secondary grades. Fall 1995 public school membership in elementary grades increased by 6,113 students (1.6%) over fall 1994 and by 26,822 students (7.7%) over fall 1991. In secondary grades public school membership increased by 9,645 students (3.5%) over fall 1994 and 36,427 students (14.8%) over fall 1991. For the sixth consecutive year, Colorado's student membership has shown a substantial increase from the previous year.
- Between 1991 and 1995 fall membership in public schools increased by 31,542 (21.2%) for minority students and 31,707 (7.1%) for white students. Native Americans, Asians, Blacks and Hispanics represented 27.5 percent of the fall 1995 student membership, while these same groups made up 9.0 percent of Colorado's teachers.
- The ten largest school districts in 1995 increased their membership by 9,053 students (2.6%) over fall 1994 and 31,090 students (9.5%) over fall 1991. Colorado's ten largest public school districts account for 358,154 (54.5%) of the total student membership in fall 1995. Of the total increase from fall 1991 to fall 1995, the ten largest school districts in 1995 account for 49.1 percent of that growth.
- The Denver Metropolitan Area includes 351,621 (53.5%) of the state's total student membership in public schools.
- The fall 1995 pupil/teacher ratio of 18.5 to 1 increased from 18.4 to 1 in fall 1994. When special education and special subject teachers are excluded the ratio was 24.7 to 1 in fall 1995.
- Colorado's graduation rate for Class of 1995 was 77.4 percent. This is a decrease of 1.4 percentage points from rates reported for Class of 1994. The 1995 graduation rate is a four-year rate based on students reported by school districts beginning 9th grade in the 1991-92 school year.
