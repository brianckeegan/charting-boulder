

# INTRODUCTION

Information used to prepare this publication was collected from Colorado's public school districts.

Many student membership tables contained in this publication are similar to those published in prior years. The dropout rate included in Table 2 is for grades 10-12 only. Additional information on dropout and graduation rates is available in a separate publication titled Colorado Graduation Rates for Class of 1994 and 1993-94 Annual Dropout Rates for Grades 7-12.

# HIGHLIGHTS:

- In fall 1994, there were 640,521 students in Colorado's public schools, an increase of 15,459 students (2.5%) over the fall 1993 membership of 625,062. For the fifth consecutive year, Colorado's student membership has shown a substantial increase from the previous year. Fall membership grew by 77,766 students (13.8%) from fall 1989 to fall 1994.
- Beginning with fall 1990, membership each year has surpassed the previous membership peak of 1972. Colorado's public school membership declined 5.6 percent between 1972 and 1983 before beginning an upward trend. From fall 1983 to fall 1994 student membership increased by 98,325 students (18.1%).
- Membership continues to rise in both elementary and secondary grades. Fall 1994 public school membership in elementary grades increased by 6,292 students (1.7%) over fall 1993 and by 42,800 students (13.1%) over fall 1989. In secondary grades public school membership increased by 9,167 students (3.5%) over fall 1993 and 34,966 students (14.8%) over fall 1989.
- Between 1989 and 1994 fall membership in public schools increased by 32,686 (23.8%) for minority students and 45,080 (10.6%) for white students. Native Americans, Asians, Blacks and Hispanics represented 26.5 percent of the fall 1994 student membership, while these same groups made up 9.0 percent of Colorado's teachers.
- The ten largest school districts in 1994 increased their membership by 7,869 students (2.3%) over fall 1993 and 44,378 students (14.6%) over fall 1989. Colorado's ten largest public school districts account for 349,101 (54.5%) of the total student membership in fall 1994. Of the total increase from fall 1989 to fall 1994, the ten largest school districts in 1994 account for 57.1 percent of that growth.
- The Denver Metropolitan Area includes 342,787 (53.5%) of the state's total student membership in public schools.
- The fall 1994 pupil/teacher ratio of 18.4 to 1 decreased from 18.6 to 1 in fall 1993. When special education and special subject teachers are excluded the ratio was 24.5 to 1 in fall 1994.
- Colorado's graduation rate for Class of 1994 was 78.8 percent. This is a decrease of 1.1 percentage points from rates reported for Class of 1992 and 1993. The 1994 graduation rate is a four-year rate based on students reported by school districts beginning 9th grade in the 1990-91 school year.
