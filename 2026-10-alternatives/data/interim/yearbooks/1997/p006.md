

# INTRODUCTION

Information used to prepare this publication was collected from Colorado's public school districts.

Many student membership tables contained in this publication are similar to those published in prior years. The dropout rate included in Table 2 is for grades 10-12 only. Additional information on dropout and graduation rates is available in a separate publication titled Colorado Graduation Rates for Class of 1997 and 1996-97 Annual Dropout Rates for Grades 7-12.

# HIGHLIGHTS:

- In fall 1997, there were 687,167 students in Colorado's public schools, an increase of 13,729 students (2.0%) over the fall 1996 membership of 673,438. Fall membership grew by 62,105 students (9.9%) from fall 1993 to fall 1997.
- Beginning with fall 1990, membership each year has surpassed the previous membership peak of 1972. Colorado's public school membership declined 5.6 percent between 1972 and 1983 before beginning an upward trend. From fall 1984 to fall 1997 student membership increased by 141,740 students (26.0%).
- Membership continues to rise in both elementary and secondary grades. Fall 1997 public school membership in elementary grades increased by 5,873 students (1.5%) over fall 1996 and by 30,216 students (8.4%) over fall 1992. In secondary grades public school membership increased by 7,856 students (2.7%) over fall 1996 and 44,316 students (17.4%) over fall 1992. For the seventh consecutive year, Colorado's student membership has shown a substantial increase from the previous year.
- Between 1993 and 1997 fall membership in public schools increased by 35,117 (21.7%) for minority students and 26,988 (5.8%) for white students. Native Americans, Asians, Blacks and Hispanics represented 28.7 percent of the fall 1997 student membership, while these same groups made up 9.2 percent of Colorado's teachers.
- The ten largest school districts in 1997 increased their membership by 9,395 students (2.5%) over fall 1996 and 40,447 students (12.0%) over fall 1992. Colorado's ten largest public school districts account for 377,909 (55.0%) of the total student membership in fall 1997. Of the total increase from fall 1992 to fall 1997, the ten largest school districts in 1997 account for 54.3 percent of that growth.
- The Denver Metropolitan Area includes 371,327 (54.0%) of the state's total student membership in public schools.
- The fall 1997 pupil/teacher ratio of 18.2 decreased 0.3 percentage points from fall 1996. When special education and special subject teachers are excluded the ratio was 24.0 to 1 in fall 1997.
- Colorado's graduation rate for Class of 1997 was 78.5 percent. This is an increase of 0.8 percentage points from rates reported for Class of 1996. The 1997 graduation rate is a four-year rate based on students reported by school districts beginning 9th grade in the 1993-94 school year.
