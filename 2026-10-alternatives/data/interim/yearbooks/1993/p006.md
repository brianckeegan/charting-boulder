

# INTRODUCTION

Information used to prepare this publication was collected from Colorado's public school districts.

Many student membership tables contained in this publication are similar to those published in prior years. The dropout rate included in Table 2 is for grades 10-12 only. Additional information on dropout and graduation rates is available in a separate publication titled Colorado Graduation Rates for Class of 1993 and 1992-93 Annual Dropout Rates for Grades 7-12.

# HIGHLIGHTS:

- In fall 1993, there were 625,062 students in Colorado's public schools. For the fourth consecutive year, Colorado's student membership has shown a substantial increase from the previous year. Fall 1990 membership increased by 11,458 students (2.0%), fall 1991 membership increased by 18,817 students (3.3%), fall 1992 membership increased by 19,605 students (3.3%) and fall 1993 membership increased by 12,427 students (2.0%).
- School membership in fall 1993 was the largest in Colorado's history. The 1991, 1992 and 1993 student memberships surpassed the previous membership peak in fall 1972. Colorado's public school membership declined 5.6 percent between 1972 and 1983 before beginning an upward trend.
- Fall 1993 public school membership in elementary grades increased by 4,612 students (1.3%) over fall 1992 and by 76,867 (26.9%) over fall 1983. Membership in secondary grades declined by 19,800 students (7.7%) during the period from fall 1983 to fall 1989. However, membership has increased in secondary grades as the larger elementary grade membership progresses to the secondary grades. Fall 1993 membership in secondary grades increased by 7,815 students (3.1%) over fall 1992 and increased by 25,799 students (10.9%) during the four year period from fall 1989 to fall 1993.
- Between 1983 and 1993 fall membership in public schools increased by 41,935 (35.0%) for minority students, 40,931 (9.7%) for white students and 82,866 (15.3%) overall. Native Americans, Asians, Blacks and Hispanics represented 25.9 percent of the fall 1993 student membership, while these same groups made up 9.0 percent of Colorado's teachers.
- Membership in the ten largest public school districts increased by 4,004 (1.2%) over fall 1992 and 30,516 (9.8%) over fall 1988. Colorado's ten largest public school districts, each with student membership over 18,000, account for 341,466 (54.6%) of the total student membership. Fall membership in Colorado increased by 64,981 students (11.6%) since 1988. The ten largest school districts account for 47.0 percent of that growth.
- The Denver Metropolitan Area includes 336,007 (53.8%) of the state's total student membership in public schools.
- The fall 1993 pupil/teacher ratio of 18.6 to 1 increased from 18.3 to 1 in fall 1992. For 1990 and 1991, the pupil/teacher ratio was 17.8 to 1 and 17.9 to 1 respectively. From 1990 to 1993 the pupil/teacher ratio has steadily increased. This is a reflection of the large growth in student population during those periods. When special education and special subject teachers are excluded the ratio was 24.7 to 1 in fall 1993.
- Colorado's graduation rate for Class of 1993 was 79.9 percent. This is the same rate reported for Class of 1992. The Class of 1991 had a 78.9 percent graduation rate. The 1993 graduation rate is a four-year rate based on students reported by school districts beginning 9th grade in the 1989-90 school year.
