

# INTRODUCTION

Information used to prepare this publication was collected from Colorado's public school districts.

Many student membership tables contained in this publication are similar to those published in prior years. The dropout rate included in Table 2 is for grades 7-12. Additional information on dropout and graduation rates is available in a separate publication titled Colorado Graduation Rates for Class of 1998 and 1997-98 Annual Dropout Rates for Grades 7-12.

# HIGHLIGHTS:

- In fall 1998, there were 699,135 students in Colorado's public schools, an increase of 11,968 students (1.7%) over the fall 1997 membership of 687,167. Fall membership grew by 58,614 students (9.2%) from fall 1994 to fall 1998.
- Beginning with fall 1990, membership each year has surpassed the previous membership peak of 1972. Colorado's public school membership declined 5.6 percent between 1972 and 1983 before beginning an upward trend. From fall 1984 to fall 1998 student membership increased by 153,708 students (28.2%).
- Membership continues to rise in both elementary and secondary grades. Fall 1998 public school membership in elementary grades increased by 4,752 students (1.2%) over fall 1997 and by 34,968 students (6.6%) over fall 1994. In secondary grades public school membership increased by 7,216 students (2.4%) over fall 1997 and 34,550 students (12.7%) over fall 1994. For the seventh consecutive year, Colorado's student membership has shown a substantial increase from the previous year.
- Between 1994 and 1998 fall membership in public schools increased by 36,045 (21.2%) for minority students and 22,569 (4.8%) for white students. Native Americans, Asians, Blacks and Hispanics represented 29.4 percent of the fall 1998 student membership.
- The ten largest school districts in 1998 increased their membership by 8,246 students (2.2%) over fall 1997 and 37,054 students (10.6%) over fall 1994. Colorado's ten largest public school districts accounted for 386,155 (55.2%) of the total student membership in fall 1998. Of the total increase from fall 1994 to fall 1998, the ten largest school districts in 1998 accounted for 63.2 percent of that growth.
- The Denver Metropolitan Area includes 380,233 (54.4%) of the state's total student membership in public schools.
- Colorado's graduation rate for Class of 1998 was 80.1 percent. This is an increase of 1.6 percentage points from rates reported for Class of 1997. The 1998 graduation rate is a four-year rate based on students reported by school districts beginning 9th grade in the 1994-95 school year.
