

# INTRODUCTION

Information used to prepare this publication was collected from Colorado's public school districts.

Many student membership reports contained in this publication are similar to those published in prior years. The dropout rate included in Table 2 is for grades 10-12 only. Additional information on dropout and graduation rates is available in a separate publication titled Colorado Graduation Rates for Class of 1992 and 1991-92 Annual Dropout Rates for Grades 7-12.

# HIGHLIGHTS:

- In fall 1992, there were 612,635 students in Colorado's public schools. This is the third consecutive year that Colorado's student membership has shown a substantial increase from the previous year. Fall 1990 membership increased by 11,458 students (2.0%), fall 1991 membership increased by 18,817 students (3.3%) and fall 1992 membership increased by 19,605 students (3.3%).
- School membership in fall 1992 was the largest in Colorado's history. The 1991 and 1992 student membership surpassed the previous membership peak in fall 1972. Colorado's public school membership declined 5.6 percent between 1972 and 1983 before beginning an upward trend.
- Fall 1992 public school membership in elementary grades increased by 10,239 students (2.9%) over fall 1991 and by 72,255 (25.3%) over fall 1983. Membership in secondary grades declined by 19,800 students (7.7 %) during the period from fall 1983 to fall 1989. However, membership has increased in secondary grades as the larger elementary grade membership progresses to the secondary grades. Fall 1992 membership in secondary grades increased by 9,366 students (3.8%) over fall 1991 and increased by 17,984 students (7.6%) during the three year period from fall 1989 to fall 1992.
- Between 1983 and 1992 fall membership in public schools increased by 30.2 percent for minority students, 8.1 percent for white students and 13.0 percent overall. Native Americans, Asians, Blacks and Hispanics represented 25.5 percent of the fall 1992 student membership, while these same groups made up 9.0 percent of Colorado's teachers.
- Membership in the ten largest public school districts increased by 10,398 (3.2%) over fall 1991 and 26,512 (8.5%) over fall 1988. Colorado's ten largest public school districts, with student membership over 17,000, account for 337,462 (55.1%) of the total student membership. Fall membership in Colorado increased 52,554 students (9.4%) since 1988. The ten largest school districts account for 50.4 percent of that growth.
- The Denver Metropolitan Area includes 330,248 (53.9 %) of the state's total student membership in public schools.
- The fall 1992 pupil/teacher ratio of 18.3 to 1 increased from 17.9 to 1 in fall 1991. This is a reflection of the large growth in student population. When special education and special subject teachers are excluded the ratio is 24.4 to 1.
- Colorado's graduation rate for Class of 1992 was 79.9 percent. This represents an increase of 1.0 percentage points from the 1991 graduation rate of 78.9 percent. The graduation rate is a four-year rate based on students reported by school districts beginning 9th grade in the 1988-89 school year.
