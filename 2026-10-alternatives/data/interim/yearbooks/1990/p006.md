

# INTRODUCTION

This report has been prepared from information collected from Colorado's public school districts.

Many of the pupil membership reports are similar to those we have published in prior years. In addition to providing high school graduates by district, this report includes graduation rates for the Class of 1990. The dropout rate included in Table 2 is for grades 10, 11 and 12 only. Additional information on dropout rates and graduation rates is available in a separate publication titled "Colorado Public School Annual Dropout Rates - Grades Seven Through Twelve 1989-90 and Class of 1990 Graduation Rates."

# HIGHLIGHTS:

- In the fall of 1990 there were 574,213 students enrolled in Colorado's public schools, an increase of 11,458 students over the 1989 fall membership of 562,755. This represented the largest increase since 1971 when fall membership increased by 14,422.
- Membership in grades kindergarten through sixth increased 2.9 percent over fall 1989 and 15.7 percent over fall 1981. Grades seven through twelve membership showed a slight increase (.01 percent) over fall 1989, but decreased 6.5 percent from fall 1981.
- Colorado's public school membership declined 4.7 percent between 1975 and 1983 before beginning an upward trend. Between 1983 and 1990 fall membership increased by 18.2 percent for minority students, 2.4 percent for white students and 5.9 percent overall.
- Native Americans, Asians, Blacks and Hispanics represented 24.7 percent of the total student population, while these same groups made up 9.2 percent of Colorado's teachers.
- The pupil/teacher ratio in Colorado for fall 1990 was 17.8 to 1. However, when special education and special subject teachers were excluded, the ratio became 23.6 to 1.
- The Denver Metropolitan Area includes 307,847 (53.6 percent) of the total state's pupil membership.
