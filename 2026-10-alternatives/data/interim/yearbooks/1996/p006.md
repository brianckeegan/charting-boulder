

# INTRODUCTION

Information used to prepare this publication was collected from Colorado's public school districts.

Many student membership tables contained in this publication are similar to those published in prior years. The dropout rate included in Table 2 is for grades 10-12 only. Additional information on dropout and graduation rates is available in a separate publication titled Colorado Graduation Rates for Class of 1996 and 1995-96 Annual Dropout Rates for Grades 7-12.

# HIGHLIGHTS:

- In fall 1996, there were 673,438 students in Colorado's public schools, an increase of 17,159 students (2.6%) over the fall 1995 membership of 656,279. Fall membership grew by 60,803 students (9.9%) from fall 1992 to fall 1996.
- Beginning with fall 1990, membership each year has surpassed the previous membership peak of 1972. Colorado's public school membership declined 5.6 percent between 1972 and 1983 before beginning an upward trend. From fall 1984 to fall 1996 student membership increased by 128,011 students (23.5%).
- Membership continues to rise in both elementary and secondary grades. Fall 1996 public school membership in elementary grades increased by 7,326 students (2.0%) over fall 1995 and by 24,001 students (6.7%) over fall 1992. In secondary grades public school membership increased by 9,833 students (3.5%) over fall 1995 and 36,802 students (14.5%) over fall 1992. For the seventh consecutive year, Colorado's student membership has shown a substantial increase from the previous year.
- Between 1992 and 1996 fall membership in public schools increased by 32,169 (20.6%) for minority students and 28,634 (6.3%) for white students. Native Americans, Asians, Blacks and Hispanics represented 28.0 percent of the fall 1996 student membership, while these same groups made up 9.0 percent of Colorado's teachers.
- The ten largest school districts in 1996 increased their membership by 10,360 students (2.9%) over fall 1995 and 31,052 students (9.2%) over fall 1992. Colorado's ten largest public school districts account for 368,514 (54.7%) of the total student membership in fall 1996. Of the total increase from fall 1992 to fall 1996, the ten largest school districts in 1996 account for 51.1 percent of that growth.
- The Denver Metropolitan Area includes 361,705 (53.7%) of the state's total student membership in public schools.
- The fall 1996 pupil/teacher ratio of 18.5 to 1 did not change from fall 1995. When special education and special subject teachers are excluded the ratio was 24.5 to 1 in fall 1996.
- Colorado's graduation rate for Class of 1996 was 77.7 percent. This is an increase of 0.3 percentage points from rates reported for Class of 1995. The 1996 graduation rate is a four-year rate based on students reported by school districts beginning 9th grade in the 1992-93 school year.
