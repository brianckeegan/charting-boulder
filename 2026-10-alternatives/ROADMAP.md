## Added after the build — 2026-09-17

- [x] **The teacher-FTE PDFs** — done. The CDE school-grain series went from three years to eleven: 2003, 2004, 2013–2016, 2018, 2019, 2022–2024. A district series from NCES, 1987–2024, came with it.
- [x] **2005–2012 teacher FTE** — done, and 2000–2004 with it. The CDE school-grain series now runs 2000–2024, twenty-two years. No OCR was needed: the text was there all along behind a rotated page, an unhandled line operator and a page whose content is split across several stream objects.
- [x] **District-grain teacher FTE from CDE** — done. `district-teacher-fte-cde.csv` is 4,037 rows over 2000–2024, carrying CDE's own district total where it publishes one and the sum of the district's schools everywhere.
- [x] **The average-salary reports** — done, and the answer is that **2017 does not exist**. The volumes are parsed now: 2016 gives 197 districts and 52,079 FTE against the archive's 51,461 from the school sums, and 2018 gives 195, every row checked against the two categories that must add to it. The 2017 volume gives the same 197 districts and the same 52,079.2 FTE to the decimal — CDE published 2016 again at the 2017 URL, as it did with the ratio report, except that this file is not byte-identical so the checksum could not catch it. A year can repeat another without repeating its bytes, and that is checked for now.
- [ ] **The 2013 and 2019 salary volumes** — 2013 prints its figures run together with the district name ("ADAMS 12 FIVE STAR SCHOOLS2,092.7$54,052.54$293.76") and 2019 extracts with its cells merged, so the parts-must-add check throws its rows out and only 154 of 185 districts survive. Both years already have a ratio report, so this buys a second reading rather than a year.
- [ ] **The membership-serial ratio PDFs for 2000–2002 and 2004** — still extract no text at all, and 2003's and 2012's staff reports lose about 200 rows each. Every one of those years is covered by the other serial, so this buys a second reading rather than a year.
- [x] **The yearbooks' summary table** — done. Printed as Table 1 in some volumes and Table 2 in others, which is why the roadmap had the number wrong. It gives district staffing and school counts for 1986–1999 and takes `district-teacher-fte-cde.csv` from 4,037 rows to 6,695, spanning 1986–2024. Every year from 1987 to 1998 reconciles to NCES within 1.3%.
- [ ] **1988** — the one yearbook volume whose *enrollment* does not reconcile against NCES, at −0.70%, with three genuine row-total failures. Every other volume is within 0.16%. Its staffing, read from a different table in the same volume, reconciles at +0.7%, so whatever is wrong is in the by-grade table rather than the scan.
- [ ] **Graduation and dropout rates, 1986–1999** — parsed and carried in `district-teacher-fte-cde.csv`, but **unchecked**: no second source in this archive holds them. CDE publishes dropout and graduation series separately, which would give them the same treatment every other column here gets.
- [x] **The 384 unmatched yearbook district-years** — done, and it found two mis-attributions on the way (D27), and the audit of 2026-09-23 found a third: 1989–1995 had summed Rifle and Parachute into one "GARFIELD" row (D32). 98.4% of district-years now carry a code, and 1977–85 joins the archive for the first time. What is left is 78 non-BOCES rows where one printed name covers two districts (East/West Yuma County) or the district merged away before 2000, plus the BOCES, which are not districts. Each is named in `DATA-DICTIONARY.md`.
- [x] **2001–2003 on the CDE side** — done, and it filled a hole rather than buying a second source: `district-year` ran 1977–1999 and then 2004–2024, and now runs 1977–2024 missing only 2000. `pipeline/membership.py` reads the two PDFs from character positions and the 2003 panel by its indentation. Every row's grades add to its printed total, every district's schools add to its printed district total but one, and against NCES the two PDF years agree **to the pupil** once pre-kindergarten is excluded.
- [ ] **2000 on the CDE side** — the one year that cannot be filled. CDE published no school-by-grade file for Fall 2000 at all; the inventory has 1,065 membership files for that year and none of them is the count. NCES covers it.
- [ ] **Home-based education** — CDE publishes it for Fall 1998 to Fall 2015 as trend tables rather than a yearly count, so it needs a different reading from the non-public files. It bears on the same question they do: where pupils went when a school closed.
- [ ] **Non-public membership, 2015–2017** — the 2015 file drops the header the other twelve years carry, and 2016's URL is a 404. 2003–2014 are read.
- [ ] **Charter schools before 1998** — `is_charter` comes from NCES, which starts carrying the flag in 1998, at 69 schools. Colorado's charter law is 1993, so the first five years are missing. CDE's own charter history would close it.

## Added after the analysis — 2026-09-18

- [x] **The Resilient Schools proposal** — supplied by hand and committed to `data/raw/proposal/`: resolution 26-27, the 15 September 2026 work session deck, and the district's own metrics.
- [x] **BVSD capacity by school** — done, from the district's own ArcGIS attendance-area layer rather than the deck, which charts capacity for two schools and tables it for none. The layer carries it for all 31 buildings, at its February 2026 vintage, and the levers are scored against it.
- [x] **City of Boulder neighbourhood boundaries** — supplied by hand as the ten city subcommunities. 16,195 of the district's 41,063 children live inside them; the rest are in Louisville, Lafayette, Superior, Broomfield and the mountains, where the places layer carries the geography.
- [x] **A catchment model that matches both its checks** — closed by dropping the model. Straight-line nearest-school got Mesa within 6% of the district's resident count and Bear Creek at 45%; the district's real attendance-area polygons replaced it, so there is nothing left to check. What remains is the child-to-pupil ratio, still flat district-wide because PL 94-171 gives under-18 by block and no finer.
- [x] **Resident children by single year of age, by block** — done, and better than proposed. The 2020 DHC publishes **P12, sex by age in bands, down to the block**, so the 5-to-17 count per attendance area is measured rather than apportioned: 31,937 of the district's 41,063 under-18s. Every one of the 7,193 blocks agrees with PL 94-171 on population and on under-18 exactly, which is the check on the file offsets. Single years do stop at the tract, so the elementary split is PCT12's tract profile carried onto its blocks, and both a 5-to-10 and a 5-to-11 band are carried because K-5 spans them.
- [x] **Open enrollment in the catchments** — done. `pipeline/oe_matrix.py` reads the three 2025-26 Enrollment Pattern Matrices from `2026-09-bvsd/data/raw/open-enrollment/`, where they were already committed, and all 69 school rows rebuild to their printed totals exactly. Section 4 of the analysis separates a school that is small because its area emptied from one that is small because its families left.
- [x] **Open enrollment as a trend** — done. All thirty matrices, 2016-17 to 2025-26, now read. Neither answer the question offered: the share attending their own neighbourhood school held at about 70% to 2020, broke in 2021, and has fallen every year since to 62.6%. Of the 2,530 pupils the neighbourhood schools have lost, 1,848 are demography and 682 are families leaving.
- [x] **A statewide steady state** — done, in section 6, and the answer is no. Run for all 178 districts on their own county forecasts, Boulder Valley ranks **73rd of 178** by implied contraction to 2060; 72 districts face a larger one. Cross-sectionally its average school of 500 pupils is the **8th smallest of the 20 districts with 10,000 pupils or more**, against a peer median of 573. The cross-section deliberately uses no fitted form: over a range from 25 pupils to 90,450 a log-log line puts Denver 60% above its own norm, which says something about the functional form and nothing about Denver.
- [x] **Closure impacts** — done, in section 7. Thirty-three districts have run fifty-nine closure rounds since 1992, Boulder Valley's 2004 among them. A closure round cuts the school count about 17% and holds it 15% down four years on, and moves neither enrolment nor staffing at any horizon, with intervals ruling out an enrolment fall of more than about 3% in the three years after it. **A closure round changes the number of buildings, not the number of pupils or teachers.**
- [x] **County population before 1990** — closed as far as it can be. The State Demography Office publishes county and municipal population annually from 1980 and decennially from 1870, and both are read into `data/analysis/county-population.csv` and `municipal-population.csv`. It is **total population, not by age**: the single-year-of-age file still starts in 1990 and the district panel still starts with it. The two SDO products agree to a median 0.015%, and 97% of county-years are within half a per cent.
- [x] **A closure event study that handles staggered timing** — done, in section 8, with a Callaway–Sant'Anna estimator: 18 cohorts against districts not yet treated, 1,000 draws resampling whole districts. The flat enrolment path survives (+0.3% at the round, interval −1.1% to +1.6%) and the buildings effect gets **stronger**, because section 7's fade to 10.4% by the fifth year was the comparison group, not the districts. On clean comparisons it is still 16.2% down and excludes zero (D29, D32).
- [x] **The children counted twice, twenty years apart** — done, in section 9. Both censuses on 2000 tract geometry rather than a chained crosswalk (D30). BVSD has 1.3% more school-age children than in 2000, with nineteen tracts gaining and twenty-six losing, and the two sides nearly cancelling.
- [x] **Density against children, across the Front Range** — done, and the answer is that it barely holds: rank correlation −0.12 over 1,091 tracts, flat by band from under a hundred people a square mile to ten thousand, and the sign reverses between adjacent counties. Boulder is low at every density.
- [x] **A sixth lever: fill the buildings** — done, in section 3. BVSD already draws 765 elementary pupils from outside its boundary, against the 519 it would take to lift every plains school to the bar, and that flow rose 11.2% while the roll fell 19.8%. Sixty-one years at the rate the district has actually managed, so it is a lever rather than a plan.
- [x] **Costing the combinations** — done, in section 3, against the plan the board approved on 22 September 2026 (D35–D37). The costs are counts, not dollars: schools kept, schools below the bar, schools over capacity, children moved and pupils to recruit. In 2027 the approved plan with a redraw moves 1,270 children and needs 106 recruits; keeping all 27 schools moves 642 and needs 488 by 2027 and 694 by 2030, against the 765 the district draws in today.
- [x] **Data for the published charts** — done, in section 10. `datawrapper/` holds a CSV for each draft chart, and GeoJSON for the two maps, with a README that gives each chart's type, title, source and caveat (D38).
- [ ] **Eldorado's new capacity** — Eldorado becomes a PK-5 school when its middle grades leave, and may gain elementary room. The capacity layer does not say how much, so the model keeps its February 2026 figure.
