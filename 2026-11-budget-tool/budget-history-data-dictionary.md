# Boulder city budget history — data dictionary & caveats

Generated by `budget-history.py`. Coverage: **2021–2027**, 210 observations across
70 measures, from 6 source documents. Re-run the script to regenerate.

> **The single most important thing in this file:** there are **two accounting
> bases here and they must never be charted as one series.** The `budget_*`
> headline totals and the `sources_*`/`uses_*` citywide snapshot disagree by
> **$83.4M in 2023** for structural reasons — see caveat 8.

| File | Shape | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `budget-history.csv` | tidy long | the canonical data; one row per `(year, measure, basis)` |
| `budget-history-wide.csv` | wide | chart-ready headline series, each with its `*_basis` label |
| `budget-history-provenance.csv` | sidecar | one row per source document, joined on `source_id` |

## Read this first: `basis` is load-bearing

A budget number means nothing without its vintage. The same year and measure
routinely carries three or four published values:

| 2025 sales & use tax | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| What council adopted | $180.17M | `adopted` |
| Mid-year revision | $176.84M | `revised_projection` |
| What actually came in | $178.75M | `actual` |

Those differ by $3.3M — larger than the entire 2027 budget gap. **Filter to one
`basis` before charting.** A series that silently mixes them will look like
volatility when it is really just different questions.

| `basis` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `actual` | Closed-year collections (2025 is unaudited year-end) |
| `adopted` | Approved by council for that year |
| `recommended` | City manager's proposal, not yet adopted (all 2027 figures) |
| `revised_projection` | Mid-year revision to an adopted figure |
| `total_budget` | Adopted **plus amendments and carryforward** (OpenGov snapshot, 2023) |
| `forecast` | Forward-looking model output |
| `identified` | A gap named during budget development |
| `derived` | **Computed here, not published** — see caveats |
| `policy` | A standing policy target, not a year-specific amount |

## Columns

### `budget-history.csv`

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `year` | int | Budget/fiscal year the value describes |
| `measure` | str | See the catalog below |
| `basis` | str | Vintage — see above |
| `value` | float | In `unit`; **not** inflation-adjusted |
| `unit` | str | `musd` (millions of dollars), `fte`, `pct`, `mills` |
| `source_id` | str | Joins to `budget-history-provenance.csv` |

### Measure catalog

| Prefix | Measures | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `budget_` | `total`, `operating`, `capital`, `general_fund` | `operating + capital = total` in every year |
| `gap_` | `general_fund`, `..._low`, `..._high` | The General Fund shortfall identified for that year |
| `salesuse_` | `retail`, `rec_marijuana_addl`, `consumer_business_use`, `construction_use`, `motor_vehicle_use`, `audits_sales`, `audits_use`, `total` | Citywide, all funds |
| `property_` | `tax_revenue`, `assessed_value`, `mill_levy` | Assessed value also in `musd` |
| `revenue_` | 11 categories + `total` | **2026 only**; citywide all-funds revenue mix |
| `sources_revenue_` | 18 categories + `total` | **Different basis** — citywide gross Sources & Uses, 2021–2023 |
| `uses_expense_` | `personnel`, `capital`, `operating`, `transfers`, `internal_services`, `debt_service`, `total` | **Different basis** — the only expense composition in the dataset |
| `net_revenues_less_expenses` | — | Snapshot revenues minus expenses; negative in 2023 |
| `positions_` | `eliminated`, `..._filled`, `..._vacant`, `term_limited_ending`, `frozen_to_2028`, `added` | **Changes, not levels** |
| `yoy_` | `total_pct`, `operating_pct`, `general_fund_pct`, `capital_pct` | As published by the city |
| `reserve_policy_pct_of_operating` | — | Standing policy target (16.7%) |
| `salesuse_component_residual` | — | See caveats |

## Caveats

**1. 2025 General Fund is derived, not published.**
`budget_general_fund` for 2025 (`basis = derived`, $211.0M) was back-computed as
`194.5 / (1 − 0.078)` from the city's statement that 2026 fell 7.8% from 2025.
The 7.8% is itself rounded, so treat this as ±$1M and **do not quote it as a
city figure.** Every other General Fund value is published.

**2. Two different property-tax figures for 2026, and they are not errors.**
`property_tax_revenue` (adopted) is **$59.17M** — the city's own 11.648-mill
levy. `revenue_property_tax` (from the citywide revenue mix) is **$61.73M**,
a broader all-funds scope. They answer different questions; don't compare them
directly or chart them as one series.

**3. Component sums miss their published totals by a few hundredths.**
The city totals at full precision and publishes components rounded to $0.01M,
so a column of six components can legitimately miss its own total. These
residuals are recorded explicitly as `salesuse_component_residual` (range
−0.02 to +0.04) rather than hidden. The published total is authoritative.
`budget-history.py` fails the build if any residual exceeds $0.05M.

**4. Staffing is changes, not levels.**
The sources publish positions eliminated/added per year, never a headcount.
There is no FTE *level* series here, and one cannot be derived from these
rows. Recovering it requires the personnel schedules in the budget books.

**5. Nominal dollars throughout.** No inflation adjustment. Year-over-year
changes across 2022–2027 partly reflect inflation, which was substantial early
in this window.

**6. All 2027 values are `recommended`.** Council's first reading was Oct 1,
2026 and final vote Oct 15, 2026. Figures may have changed on adoption — check
before publishing anything that depends on them.

**7. Headline coverage starts at 2022.** The `budget_*` totals begin in 2023
(2022 has sales-tax actuals only). The council packets chart budget history to
2019, but those totals appear only inside chart images.

**8. Two accounting bases — do not merge them.**
The `sources_*`/`uses_*` family comes from the OpenGov citywide Sources & Uses
export and is **not** comparable to the `budget_*` headline totals:

| 2023 | `uses_expense_total` | `budget_total` | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| | $598.79M | $515.40M | **+$83.39M** |

Two structural reasons, not an error in either:

1. **Different vintage.** The snapshot's 2023 column is *Total Budget* —
   adopted plus amendments and carryforward — while $515.4M is the *adopted*
   figure. (The snapshot's 2022 column is adopted, and 2021 is actuals: three
   bases in one file, which is why each carries its own `basis`.)
2. **Gross, not net.** Interfund flows are left in on both sides — `transfers_in`,
   `intragovernmental_charges` and `cost_allocation` on the revenue side,
   `transfers` and `internal_services` on the expense side — so money moving
   between city funds is counted more than once. Removing just transfers and
   internal services closes about $75M of the $83M gap; the rest is the
   amendment/carryforward difference.

Charting the two families together produces a fake ~$83M cliff between 2023 and
2024. **Pick one family per chart.** Use `budget_*` for the headline story and
`uses_expense_*` for composition.

**9. Snapshot revenue categories are a different taxonomy** from the `revenue_*`
family (18 categories vs 11; the snapshot has `transfers_in`, `cost_allocation`
and `intragovernmental_charges`, which the 2026 mix has no equivalent for).
Don't join them on category name.

## Recipes

**pandas — one basis, wide for charting**

```python
import pandas as pd
df = pd.read_csv("budget-history.csv")

totals = (df[(df.measure == "budget_total") & (df.basis.isin(["adopted", "recommended"]))]
            .pivot_table(index="year", values="value"))

# Sales & use tax composition, actuals only
comp = (df[df.measure.str.startswith("salesuse_")
           & (df.measure != "salesuse_total")
           & (df.measure != "salesuse_component_residual")
           & (df.basis == "actual")]
          .pivot_table(index="year", columns="measure", values="value"))
```

**R / tidyverse**

```r
library(tidyverse)
df <- read_csv("budget-history.csv")
df |>
  filter(measure == "budget_total", basis %in% c("adopted", "recommended")) |>
  pivot_wider(names_from = measure, values_from = value)
```

**Straight to a chart.** Use `budget-history-wide.csv` — it is already one row
per year, and each series carries a `*_basis` column so a footnote can say which
vintage each point came from.

## Extending this

**OpenGov exports CSV — this is the route to deeper history.** The `snapshot2023`
source is a "Data Snapshot" CSV downloaded from an OpenGov transparency view.
That matters: the portal has no *public API* (documented REST paths return 404),
but each view exports clean, to-the-dollar CSV from the UI. Every additional
year is a download, not a PDF transcription.

To extend the series backward, open a Sources & Uses view for an earlier budget
year and export it the same way:

```
https://cityofboulderco.opengov.com/transparency/#/<dataset-id>
    ?accountType=revenuesVersusExpenses&breakdown=types&year=<year>
```

Each export carries three columns (prior-year actual, current adopted, current
total budget), so **one download adds up to three years**. The 2023 view
(dataset `65843`) reached back to 2021 actuals; a 2020 or 2017 view should reach
proportionally further.

Two things to watch when adding one:

- **Record the column labels as `basis`.** They differ per export — "Actual",
  "Adopted Budget", "Total Budget" are three different things (caveat 8).
- **Keep them in the `sources_`/`uses_` family.** Do not fold them into
  `budget_*`, however tempting the matching year looks.

**Still not available from OpenGov:** FTE *levels*. Sources & Uses carries
`uses_expense_personnel` (payroll dollars), which is a reasonable proxy for
staffing cost but is not a headcount. A true FTE series needs the personnel
schedules in the PDF budget books.

**Adding a year:** add the figures to the appropriate block in
`budget-history.py` with a `src` id, add the document to `SOURCES`, and re-run.
Reconciliation will catch a mistyped component — it checks every published total
against the sum of its parts, to the dollar for the snapshot data.
